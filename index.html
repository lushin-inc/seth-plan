The three rules
Rule 1Go from one name to four at every GROW site›
Deer Creek has run 20 projects this year because people there know you. Almost every other site runs through a single mid-level contact — and SSBO stalled the day Jason left.
  • Every quote, install, or site visit ends with one intro ask. One per visit, every visit.
  • Target the seats that fund and fix: maintenance manager, facilities manager, plant manager, plant engineer. Your EHS contacts flag hazards — these people pay to fix them.
  • Walk the floor every time you're on site. Leave a punch list: what's urgent, what can wait, roughly what each costs.
The intro ask

"Rod, quick one — do you ever work with [name] over in facilities?"

"Have you been happy with what we've done so far?"

"I don't suppose he ever mentions [guys up on the roof / pinch points on the line]?"

"Would you be open to introducing us? Even a walk-by next time I'm on site works."

Rule 2Follow your champions to their other sites›
CoorsTek has 37 locations and you sell to one. Lamb Weston's Tri-Cities cluster is an hour from your Boardman project. International Paper has an EHS manager sitting in Beaverton. The relationship travels so you don't have to.
  • Ask every champion the sister-site question — script below. One yes opens a plant you've never seen.
  • Start with managers new in role — they inherit the old hazards and have license to spend fixing them. Twelve are flagged on your CoorsTek map.
  • Use the contact maps — CoorsTek is built; Lamb Weston and IP names are loaded; PCC and ATI pulls are queued.
  • CoorsTek's biggest campuses are in Colorado. If life takes you there someday, this account moves with you.
The sister-site ask

"Who's your counterpart at [Golden / Richland / Beaverton]?"

"If I mentioned your name and the [rooftop] project, would that be OK with you?"

"Anything I should know before I reach out?"

The new-in-role opener

"[Name] — congrats on the new role. I do safety projects at your [Hillsboro] plant — guarding, fall protection, forklift/pedestrian separation. New managers usually inherit a punch list. If you're building yours, I'll walk your floor and give you a straight read on what's worth fixing first."

Rule 3Work the 12 seed targets warm — never cold›
New logos are the slowest dollars in your book. The list is capped at 12 so it never eats the time that belongs to Rule 1 and Rule 2.
  • Every SEED touch leads with a reference story: PCC guarding for the metals plants, Columbia Forest's Boplan for the wood-products mills.
  • Ferguson Valve and Oregon Tool love you and sell into plants all over the state — they're referral engines, not revenue. Ask quarterly.
  • Ten of the twelve already know you — they were rated top-potential in January and went quiet. Reactivate before you prospect.
The referral-engine ask

"Who do you sell into that's fighting the same forklift-and-pedestrian mess [PCC] had?"

"Would you introduce me the way you'd want to be introduced?"

Where this dies
  • Disappearing into installs. April–July was your best stretch ever; August went quiet because nothing was loaded behind it. The week runs anyway.
  • Single-threading. One name per site is a risk, not a relationship. SSBO already proved it.
  • Chasing red tape. City of Portland spends when Kevin calls. Serve it — don't hunt it.
  • Touching the tail. 117 accounts get the newsletter and nothing else.

Tying it all together
  1. Every account sits in a bucket. Time goes where the bucket says.
  2. On every GROW visit: one intro ask, one floor walk, one punch list.
  3. Once a quarter, ask each champion the sister-site question.
  4. SEED stays warm and capped at 12. The tail stays on mail merge.
  5. Friday, 20 minutes: count the week, load next week.
If you remember nothing else: the next dollar is at a site where someone already trusts you — ask that person for a name.
